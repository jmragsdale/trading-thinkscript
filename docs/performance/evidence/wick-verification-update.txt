# Strategy Comparison: Displacement-Wick Indicators vs JJ Simon Mean Reversion

**Date:** 2026-06-21
**Data:** same NQ 1-min sample, 2026-04-20 → 2026-05-11 (16 sessions)
**Files analyzed:** `displacement_wick_futures_v2.pine` (most advanced), `displacement_wick_stats.pine`, plus v1/alerts variants.

---

## What these indicators are

A **different concept** from JJ Simon. JJ Simon anchors to the 9:30 RTH open and fades back to it via an FVG. The displacement-wick scripts fade a **rejected displacement candle**: a candle whose body is larger than its 100-bar standard deviation (a "displacement") *and* that prints a significant rejection wick (≥15% of range, ≥0.25× body). The trade bets the rejection reverses the move.

The `_v2` file is the most eval-ready of the set. It already bakes in the exact risk controls I recommended adding to JJ Simon:

- Breakeven stop (move to entry at +1R) — *on by default*
- Max-loss cap (3R) and conservative same-bar = loss
- Pause after 2 consecutive losses; 3-bar cooldown
- Min/max risk filters (8–40 ticks) so stop size stays sane
- **Session filter already set to 09:30–11:30 and 14:00–16:00 ET** — matches your known windows
- Dynamic ATR-based stops (1.5×ATR) instead of fixed points

`displacement_wick_stats.pine` is a stripped analysis version (1.5R, trend filter, sweep entry, no BE/cap). v1 and the alerts files are earlier/lighter variants — v2 supersedes them.

---

## ⚠️ CORRECTION (most important section)

My first pass measured the **v2 indicator with all its default throttles on**, which badly understated both the trade count and the edge. The signal itself fires often; three default settings were strangling it:

- **Auto "trend gate"** — only allowed fading *counter-trend* pops. For a *mean-reversion* strategy this fights the premise and was the single biggest drag.
- **Zone-Touch retest entry** + **one-trade-at-a-time** — a setup blocks all others until price retests it; many never do.
- **40-tick (10pt) max-risk cap** — rejects nearly every 5/15-min setup because 1.5×ATR stops are 60–106pt there.

**Funnel on 5-min, one month:** 368 displacement candles → 175 with rejection wick → 60 after the trend gate → only a handful actually filled under v2's throttles.

### Corrected backtest — traded as pure displacement mean-reversion (every signal, immediate entry)

| Timeframe | Config | Trades/mo | Win rate (BE needed) | Avg R | Net |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5-min | trend-gated, 2R, BE | 48 | 29.7% (33%) | −0.33 | −16R |
| 5-min | **no gate, 1.5R, no BE** | **72** | **54.2% (40%)** | **+0.20** | **+14.3R** |
| 15-min | trend-gated, 2R, BE | 23 | 41.2% (33%) | +0.10 | +2.2R |
| 15-min | **no gate, 1.5R, no BE** | **38** | **59.5% (40%)** | **+0.31** | **+11.4R** |

**Run as actual mean reversion (no trend gate, no breakeven, 1.5R target), the strategy is net positive on both 5-min and 15-min — and better than JJ Simon's +0.10R, at much higher frequency (2.5–4.5 trades/day).** The trend gate and breakeven stop were each hurting it (BE scratched winners that dipped to entry then ran).

**Practical constraints for the eval:**
- Stops are wide: **avg 61pt on 5-min, 106pt on 15-min** (1.5×ATR beyond the wick). **Micros (MNQ) are mandatory** — a 61pt stop is $123/micro (~16R of buffer vs the $2K drawdown) but $1,230 on a mini (>½ the drawdown in one trade).
- Higher frequency reaches $3K faster than JJ Simon's one-trade-a-day, but also more drawdown exposure — needs a proper multi-trade-per-day eval Monte Carlo (not yet run).

Caveats unchanged: ~1 month of data, port (not literal Pine), config-dependent. Validate on TradingView's stats table over 6–12 months.

### Eval pass-rate Monte Carlo (corrected profile, 20k sims, 30-day window)

Multi-trade-per-day model, bootstrapping whole trading days from the real backtest, run through full Lucid Flex 50K rules (intraday breach + EOD trailing drawdown + 50% consistency + 2-day minimum). Profile: no trend gate, 1.5R, no breakeven.

**5-min (4.5 trades/day, $123 risk/micro):**

| Micros | $risk/trade | Pass% | Blowup% | Consistency-fail% | Ran-out-of-time% | Median days |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 123 | 49.3% | 0.3% | 0.0% | 50.5% | 16 |
| **2** | 246 | **84.4%** | 9.5% | 6.2% | 6.2% | 9 |
| 3 | 369 | 19.6% | 21.1% | **59.1%** | 0.2% | 6 |
| 4 | 492 | 8.7% | 29.8% | 61.5% | 0.0% | 4 |

**15-min (2.5 trades/day, $213 risk/micro):**

| Micros | $risk/trade | Pass% | Blowup% | Consistency-fail% | Ran-out-of-time% | Median days |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 213 | 51.1% | 17.9% | 0.0% | 31.0% | 13 |
| 2 | 426 | 27.0% | 38.4% | 34.3% | 0.4% | 7 |

**Findings:**

- **2 micros on 5-min is the clear sweet spot: ~84% pass, <10% blowup, median 9 days.** Your hunch that 1–2 micros wouldn't pass was half right — 1 micro is a coin flip (49%, but it just runs out of time, essentially never blows up), while **2 micros passes comfortably**.
- **The consistency rule — not drawdown — is the ceiling.** At 3+ micros you hit $3K so fast (4–6 days) that one big day exceeds 50% of total profit and trips the rule (59%+ fail). Sizing up *hurts* here.
- **5-min beats 15-min** for the eval: more trades, smaller per-trade risk (more R of buffer vs the $2K limit), and less blowup.
- To stay clear of the consistency rule at 2 micros, deliberately **spread the target over ≥4–5 trading days** rather than going for it in one or two sessions.

## ⚠️ VERIFICATION UPDATE — the rosy numbers did NOT hold

I added a "Market (Next Bar)" entry mode to the Pine and re-ran the port **with the indicator's real throttles included** (one-trade-at-a-time, 3-bar cooldown, 20-bar pause after 2 losses) — i.e. what you'd actually run live. The earlier +0.20R / 84%-pass came from a sandbox model that omitted those throttles and used a short lookback. With them in, the edge is fragile and mostly negative:

| Config (5-min, market entry, no gate, 1.5R, BE off) | Trades | Win rate | Avg R |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lookback 100, cooldown 3, loss-pause ON *(indicator defaults)* | 39 | 35.9% | **−0.23** |
| Lookback 100, cooldown 0, pause off | 56 | 46.4% | −0.04 |
| Lookback 20, cooldown 3, pause ON | 44 | 45.5% | +0.00 |
| Lookback 20, cooldown 0, pause off | 61 | 47.5% | +0.07 |
| *(earlier sandbox — no throttles, lookback 20)* | 72 | 54.2% | +0.20 |

Eval pass rates collapse to match: at the indicator's defaults, 2 micros = **0.2% pass / 93% blowup**. The only positive configs (+0.07 to +0.11R) require turning the indicator's own risk management OFF and using a non-default lookback — classic overfitting signs on a 1-month sample.

**Corrected conclusion: we do NOT have a trustworthy edge for displacement-wick on the available data.** The result swings from −0.23R to +0.11R on minor parameter changes. The Pine tweak works mechanically, but don't fund an eval on these numbers.

## (Superseded) First pass on 5-min and 15-min — v2 defaults

Displacement-wick is meant for higher timeframes (5–15 min), so I resampled the data and re-ran. Two problems surfaced:

1. **The default risk filters are calibrated for 1-min.** On 5/15-min, 1.5×ATR stops are much larger, so the 40-tick (10 pt) max-risk filter rejects almost every setup — the faithful default produced 0–4 trades. To get a testable count I had to widen the risk cap and shorten the warmup (noted below). That itself is a finding: **on HTF the strategy needs much larger per-trade point risk, which is harder to survive under a $2K trailing drawdown.**
2. **Still net negative**, on every timeframe and target multiple:

| Timeframe | Config | Trades | Win rate (BE needed) | Avg R | Net |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5-min | 2R | 26 | 19.0% (33%) | −0.29 | −7.5R |
| 5-min | 1.5R | 27 | 22.7% (40%) | −0.26 | −7.1R |
| 5-min | 3R | 26 | 19.0% (25%) | −0.22 | −5.8R |
| 15-min | 2R | 11 | 12.5% (33%) | −0.45 | −5.0R |
| 15-min | 1.5R | 12 | 30.0% (40%) | −0.21 | −2.5R |
| 15-min | 3R | 11 | 12.5% (25%) | −0.36 | −4.0R |

Every configuration sits **below its breakeven win rate**. The 5-min 26-trade run is the most informative sample here and it's clearly losing.

**Data limitation (important):** only ~19 sessions (~4 weeks) exist in the project, and I couldn't pull more (yfinance is network-blocked here; the FRD samples are shorter still). 11–27 trades is too thin to be the final word — but nothing in the available data suggests an edge on HTF.

## Verdict: are they better suited?

**Yes — potentially better, when run correctly (see Correction above).** As pure displacement mean-reversion on 5–15 min (no trend gate, no breakeven, 1.5R), it shows a positive edge (~54–60% win rate, +0.2 to +0.3R) at 2.5–4.5 trades/day — stronger and higher-frequency than JJ Simon. The v2 indicator's *default* settings (Auto trend + breakeven + tight risk cap + retest) are miscalibrated for a mean-reversion strategy and turned it negative; that was my first-pass error. Don't trade the defaults.

**Two big caveats keep this from being final:**

1. **Sample size.** 7–49 trades over 3 weeks proves nothing for either strategy. JJ Simon's 43.8% win rate has a 23–67% confidence interval; the same fog applies here.
2. **This is a port.** TradingView executes the real Pine with intrabar nuances my Python port approximates. The authoritative test is free: load `displacement_wick_futures_v2.pine` on a TradingView NQ/MNQ 1-min chart and read its **built-in stats table** over 6–12 months. Do that before trusting either result.

---

## Recommendation

The displacement-wick *concept* doesn't look better, but the **v2 risk architecture is its real value** — it's exactly what JJ Simon is missing. Best path:

1. **Validate both on full history first** using the v2 indicator's stats table on TradingView (months, not weeks). Cheap and decisive.
2. **If neither shows a positive edge on long history, neither is eval-worthy** — keep the capital.
3. **Most likely best outcome:** keep JJ Simon's open-anchored FVG entry (the one with positive expectancy here) but **graft on v2's risk controls** (breakeven at 1R, max-loss cap, loss-pause, ATR-aware sizing). My earlier eval test showed those controls take JJ Simon from ~37% pass / ~48% blowup to ~60% pass / ~1% blowup.
